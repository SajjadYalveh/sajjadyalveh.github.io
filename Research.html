Research Programme

Liability Gaps and Uncompensated Loss in German Private Law

This research examines situations in which loss occurs but compensation is unavailable under the applicable rules of private law. The project investigates when such situations constitute genuine liability gaps (Haftungslücken) and whether a general theory can explain the emergence of liability for otherwise uncompensated loss.

1. Point of Departure

Not every uncompensated loss constitutes a liability gap. A loss may remain uncompensated because the legal system deliberately excludes compensation, because the requirements of a particular liability rule are not satisfied, or because no applicable liability rule provides a remedy.

The research therefore begins with an independent and operational concept of uncompensated loss and then asks under what conditions a genuine Haftungslücke can be identified.

2. Conceptual Framework

The project develops the following analytical sequence:

Loss → Schaden → Ersatzfähigkeit → Haftung → Haftungslücke → Restschaden

The purpose of this framework is to distinguish the existence of loss from its legal qualification as damage, its compensability, and the existence or absence of a legal basis for liability.

3. Research Questions

The research addresses, among others, the following questions:

  • What constitutes an uncompensated loss in private law?
  • When does an uncompensated loss amount to a genuine liability gap?
  • Which forms of liability gaps can be identified in German private law?
  • What doctrinal criteria can distinguish a genuine liability gap from a deliberate exclusion of compensation?
  • Can a general theory explain the emergence of liability for uncompensated loss?
  • What comparative insights can be derived from European and Iranian private law?

4. German Private Law

The primary field of analysis is German private law, with particular attention to the law of civil liability (Deliktsrecht). The research examines statutory liability rules, their doctrinal limits, and situations in which loss remains wholly or partially uncompensated.

Particular attention is given to the concepts of Haftungslücke, nicht ersatzfähiger Schaden, ungedeckter Schaden, and, where appropriate, Restschaden.

5. Comparative Dimension

German Private Law Analysis of liability rules, doctrinal structures, and potential liability gaps within German private law.
European Private Law Comparative examination of relevant approaches in the PETL and DCFR.
Iranian Private Law Examination of Iranian civil liability rules and their treatment of losses that remain without compensation.
Imami Jurisprudence Investigation of the jurisprudential foundations of compensation, guarantee (ḍamān), and the emergence of liability.

6. Toward a General Theory

The ultimate objective is to develop a general theoretical framework for identifying and explaining liability gaps. The research asks whether, and under which normative conditions, the absence of an existing basis of liability can justify the recognition of a new basis for compensation.

The comparative dimension is intended to clarify the structural principles underlying the transition from uncompensated loss to legally recognized liability.
